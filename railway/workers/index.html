P. T. Taylor-Bailey New By DOROTHY OLAR Sault Sfar Correspondent SEARCHMONT -With the coming of the dial phone, many changes are evident in this community. One of the changes and possibly the greatest one is that the old switchboardisnowpasse. With the passing of the switchboard your friend and mine.W.P. Taylor-Bailey, will go into semi- retirement. After many years of faithful service Mr. Taylor-Bailey. or"P.T” as.he is fondly called by friends and acquaintances near and far, hopes to have time to relax and enjoy some fishing. It might, he aid, that “P. T"grew up in a railroading world. His father was in the railway supply business. His job was to upholster sleep cars and supply blinds for the coaches."P.T"entered the same career in 1905 when he Telephones Force was employed by the Canadian Pacific in Montreal. In 1916 he moved to Steelton in the employ of the Algoma Central Railway. In the many years of service he has worked in the Stores Department, as a switcher, on baggage and as station agent. As station agent he worked at Hawk Junction to return to Searchmont then back to remained here since. In1953the switchboard was moved from the station to a small house nearby and here“P.T"was on call:24 hours a day He recalls the days when Searchmont station was a very busy place. Tourist and fishermen would travel by car this far to catch ihe train and journey on up the line to their favorite camps and lakes. Now these places are easily accessible by car and so an era has passed. The Taylor-Baileys have two daughters, Enid,(Mrs. Tessier), who lives in the Sault and Marlene Mrs. Johnston) at present in Tokyo, Japan, where her husband is stationed with the Air Force, S.A.C, division.
