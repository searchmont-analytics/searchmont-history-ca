River Drivers -- Hot- Rods on Logs Algoma's Hungry Sawmills Auait The Big Push Spring Log Drive Special to the Star TORONTO-There's a sense of urge ney, an atmosphere of expectancy along the ice-filled rivers of Northern Ontario as the fabulous river-drivers await the moment the logs begin to move. Spring drives are ai most ready to start and one Canada's most spectacular jobs about to begin. True it is that trucks, tractors, railways have been carrying logs from the pulpwood and lumber camps throughout the winter. But millions of board feet of logs have been pile on the ice of lakes or or the river banks ready for the roaring waters to move them toward the mill-ponds. To keep them moving are the drivers, some of them youngsters barely out of their teens, others veterans of a score of drives, many still celebrated in song and fireside legend th rc ugh out North- ern Ontario. There's hardly a major river in the north which doesn't have a log drive. The Upper Ottawa, the Mississauga, the Marten, the Pic, Nipigon, the Aguasabon, all float their valuable cargoes lakes on which mills are situated. 10 Here the logs are boomed, held for storage, pulled up the jack ladders into the mills to be sawn into lumber or crushed, beaten dissolved’for manufacture of a thousand varieties of paper. The rush to meet a newspaper deadline isn't any greater than is the hurry to move a log cut. perfect; the moment the ice melts or goes out of lake or river, logs which have been either piled on the lake ice or along the banks of the river begin to move. Tliey must be kept moving, for only during the spring run-off, the high water, can they be kept afloat. Hang-ups-in which logs are stranded on shoals, are not only uneconomical but also call for tremendously heavy work. Log jams can be averted-sometimes by careful “feeding'’of the river. But despite man-made plans, they still happen and, in cases, they can hold up an entire drive. Hence the immediate call for skilled jam-breakers, men who can walk a rolling log as delicately and quickly as a cat, who can throw a cable loop around a key log with utmost skill or place a bundle of dynamite sticks in a strategic spot without turning a hair. Despite advances in equip ment and technique, there still hasn't been much change in the equipment used on the log drive. The load has been lessened to some extent by the use of aluminum for pike poles, the use of tractors instead of horses. But the drive still needs strong, skilled men who can wade in icy clothes from morning till night and can tell at a glance which logs are hoiding up the drive. Top river drivers can glance at a river-even a strange river -and detect unfailingly just how booms should be placed to di favourable eddy or backwater; vert logs around obstacles, into how long a drive should take, almost to the minute, including the cleaning up process which sees the drivers with their stranded logs into faster and peavies andpikes moving deeper waters.

RECALLS SOME OF ALGOMA'S FAMOUS RIVER DRIVERS Sault Star July 15 1938 Suggests PIO Games As Well As Log Rolling Contest By S.B.DICKINSON MONTREAL RIVER, July 9. (Received July 15.)-Iread an article in "The Star'a short time ago about Joe Casca net te and his ability on the river. Joe was as god a river man as ever donned a caulked shoe, but never has Joe accomplished this feat or any one else that I ever drove with. And this includes the old river drivers of both Michigan and Ontario who were second to none. I never was any front page artist on a log but I have tried this stunt and have watched many other unsuccessful attempts. When the log upend ed we did also. If The Star wishes to get the low down on Casca net te just goup to Baldwin's Mill and ask for Murdock McDonald who worked with Joe in his palmy days. The Soo district had many famous characters who followed this vocation. Here are a few: Dry Foot Sam, George McNee, whose son Jack lives on Bay Street, was one of the best white water men in the district, not so much on logs but as a forman, he understood water; Slippery Dan, a brother of Murdock; Bunk-bound Ryan Walking Daly: Seven Day Whalen. My own nickname was acquired on the Au Sable River back of Massey.I was riding a homestead, so called on account of its unusual size, and when you fall off a log bearing this cognomen you aren't much ofa driver. But I did and when I came to the surface the boss asked where I'd been.“Looking for fish tracks I replied. So the name "Fish Track Dick" stuck to me for the remainder of the season. The Star also suggested putting on a log rolling contest, why not add all of the old games played in the camps including shanty songs, poetry, step dancing, and so on.I think some of the old timers could give good entertainment. Ihave two letters which I have kept since the Great War, written from somewhere in France which I will submit later or a copy as I would not part with the originals. One is from one of the boys in the vernacular of the camps and another from our camp missionary's son who served with the boys."over there."
