The Story of Searchmont begins with Francis Hector Clergue Clergue was born in Maine and later became a businessman and entrepreneur who arrived in Sault Ste, Mariein 1894. Hecreated an industrial empire in Sault Ste. Marie and Algoma, that included a Steel Manufacturing Company, HydroElectric Power Company, Pulp and Paper Company, and Algoma Central&Hudson Bay Railway. The town of Goulais(later named Searchmont) was built asa model townsite complete with a park, which Clergue hoped would be usedto build other settlements along the railway right of way. A sawmill was built on the banks of the Goulais River, and charcoal kilns were built near the base of the existing Ski Resort. The sawmill provided lumber to build the settlement, and the charcoal kilns provided charcoal which was used in the steelmaking process.

Searchmont Personality O. Boyer, Guide, Trapper Covers 6000mile Area By DOROTHEA OLAR Sault Star Correspondent SEARCHMONT-Oscar Boyer, our personality this week is a well known figure in this area having spent a lifetime guiding and trapping from his camp at Pangis on the ACR. Winters and holidays were spent in Sault Ste. Marie or Searchmont. Mr. Boyer was born at Pangis, Oct.21,1901, where his father was in the lumbering business for Abitibi Pulp and Paper Co. His father remained with the ACR. Abi tibi Company until 1920. He took ill while still at Pangis and was unable to walk out to the track. This necessitated a month-long wait for the ice lake to board the train for the trip to the Sault. He died shortly after in hospital there. Mr. Boyer's mother, who is in her nineties, resides at a home for the aged in Toronto. Oscar has one sister, the widow of Ben Nelson who was drowned at Batch awan a River in 1944.Mrs. Nelson lives in the Sault. Mr. Boyer is well known locally as well as by those who patronized his tourist resort which he owned and operated at Pangis, Mile 69, The area covered by Boyer's expeditions, comprises6,000 sguare miles of Algoma bush land, inaccessible by car or train. The area also boasts 85 speckled trout have been taken out of here that weigh seven and three-quarter pounds. Conducted hunt in trips for moose, deer and bear in season were also offered by this famous Canadian guide and trapper. He also trained the Indian and French Canadian guides he employed. They became experts in wood lore and catering to the needs of the outdoor sportsman. This 6,000 square miles of virgin territory in Algoma's "sportsman's wonderland"has been covered from end to end by Mr. Boyer personally by canoe and portage. It is with regret that Oscar was closed on ACR property He moved to Searchmont late in 1958 with his wife, the former Blanche La France, of Pembroke, their sons Oscar, Jr. aged18 8and eight-year-old Kenny, To Mr. Boyer it was like coming back home when he moved once again to Searchmont. When he was a youngster he spent winters here with his parents, and remembers some of the oldest buildings in our town being built. He says the only church in our town the little white Roman Catholic Church on the hill, was built ir 1922 when he, along with several other fa mili ies, donated the money for the build- to strengthen when his son bookl lakes and five streams.
