Searchmont Community Centre opens A dream comes true Anewly-laid cornerstone of the Searchmont Community club is admired by vice-president, Elaine Lecuyer; president, Shirley Benedict; and secretary-treasurer, Millie Ulch. Directors are: Paul and Lois Burton, David Lecuyer, Verley Randell, Jacqueline Sisson, Melvin Sherlock, Glen Sundstrom, and Albert Tessier. BY JOHANNA BISHOP ING Special To The Star SEARCHMONTN- Nine months to a year ago, it did not seem possible, but they got it together, and hereitis, vyingfor attention with the Weldwood smoke stack: the bright, new Searchmont Community Centre, along held dream come true. About 20 years ago, Marguerite Quinn, Betty Sundstrom, Alberta Tessier Mildred Ulch and Eila Sundstrom formed the Searchmont Community Club(SCC). Mostofthe original executive members were present on Saturday to hear congratulatory messages and best wishes from Lance Bisch, representing the ministry of culture and recreation; Algoma MP Dr. Maurice Foster; Bud Wildman, Algoma MPP; Wayne Pickard, school trustee; Juhani Pulkkinen, of Weldwood and recreation committee; Marsh Barsanti.representing Sault Mayor Nick Trbovich: Al Tessier.board oi directors oftheSCC; and Rev. Father Rent, who is said to have some special connections. From their messages it became apparent what difficulties theSCCfaced in getting the centre, from mountains of red tape to financing. If there is one person in Searchmont who hasn ouse for red tape and who can wring money from a stone alias the board of education it appears to be Mildred Ul ch who was praised for her determination and perseverance by speaker after speaker. Both she and Little Al Tessier received a gift in recognition of their services. Both agreed, though, that without the massive backing of the whole community they could have accomplished little. Highlight of the opening ceremonies was the laying of the cornerstone, containing a history of the events which led to the centre. Rev. Jerry Smith led those gathered outside in prayer. The Finnish people of Sault Ste. Marie and the Scottish Dancersof Bruce Mines added a splash of color to the opening festivities as they performed to the music of their native countries. Singer Pirjo Et ela niemi delighted the audience with solo performances. She presented a hand-woven wall hanging to the SCC. Local talent added the distinctive touch that reflected the diversity of this northern community. Teacher Walter Sen ko was instrumental in getting some young artist son their way. The new kitchen facilities were used for the first time, anda stream of irresistible dishes emerged from them for a delicious buffet supper. During supper, stories were traded about the old community centre. Since 1924, it had served Searchmont, Wabos and Northland as school where Grades 1 to10were taught by a fondly remembered teacher who was not at all averse to using punishment to'enforce discipline. In the early60stheschoolbecame the community hall which was expanded in1962to include a kitchen. The kitchen addition has found a new home: Amy Eatonhas adopted it. Many people who donated their time and talent to raise money for the old and the new hall were present in Searchmont on Saturday. Among them were Larry Wagner and "Country Mama'and Dave Lezert. The old hall did have its charms, but its facilities were rather limited. When Bud Graham's Hotel burned down, the need for asocial gathering place became acute. The new centre will fulfill many duties and will become the focal point of community activities, from physical education in conjunction with the school, to a home for the bustling New Horizon club. Many groups who were hampered in their growth by a lack of space will be able to expand now and, by helping to finish the newhall and using it, they will make truly their -and their community's-own.
