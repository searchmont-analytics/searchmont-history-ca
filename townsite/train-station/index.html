His significant contributions included five patents, filed between1901and 1906, for inventions that included the Sjostedt sulphur roaster and Sjostedt electric smelting furnace. Clergue viewed the furnace asan important consumer for power from his hydro plant. According to information sent to The Sault Star this week, a lawyer for Sjostedt's estate travelled to London in the late summer of1912seeking a settlement from White Star Lines. The company refused to entertain the claim and in January 1913, a $100,000 claim (the equivalent of more than$2 million today)was filed against the ship's owners in New York. When he was in Sault Ste. Marie, Sjostedt and his family resided ina home located at 1077Queen St. E., on the south side of Queen, east of Upton Road. The home was demolished in 1985 and was a vacant lot until development of the Hadley Park subdivision, between Upton and McGregor Avenue, in the early1990s.
